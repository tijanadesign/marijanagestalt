Psychotherapy

What is psychotherapy?

Is psychotherapy for me? Does it really help? What is it like? What will we do there?

If you have ever asked yourself any of this, you are not alone. Going to psychotherapy often comes with many questions, especially the first time.

Psychotherapy is a process that helps you understand yourself better: your thoughts, feelings, reactions and behaviour. It can be a space where you speak openly about what weighs on you, without judgement and without needing to have all the answers.

Who is psychotherapy for?

Psychotherapy is for anyone who wants to understand themselves and what they are going through. You do not have to be in crisis or have a “big enough” problem to look for support.

Maybe you feel stuck, you are going through a hard period, you repeat the same patterns in relationships, or you simply want to understand your thoughts, feelings and needs better.

Do I need psychotherapy?

If something has weighed on you for a long time, if you keep going in circles or cannot find a way to make a change on your own, talking to a therapist can help.

You do not have to wait for a problem to become unbearable before looking for support.

How it works

From the first meeting to the process

How can a therapist help?

A therapist does not tell you what to do or make decisions for you.

Through conversation and questions they help you see your situation from a new perspective, recognise the patterns that hold you back and find ways to make the changes that matter to you.

What is the first meeting like?

The first meeting is a chance to get to know the therapist and to talk about what brought you to therapy.

You do not need to know in advance what you want to say. It is enough to start from whatever weighs on you most right now.

What is the therapy process like?

The therapy process is shared work that develops according to your needs and your pace. There is no single path for everyone and no script set in advance.

And what does it actually look like? We sit and talk. You can sit wherever is most comfortable, on the sofa, a chair or the floor, and take your shoes off if you want.

I ask the questions, and you do not have to know the answers in advance or where to begin. Often the answers appear through the conversation itself, sometimes by the end of the session, sometimes later.

FAQ

What people ask before their first visit

How long does therapy last?

There is no set number of meetings. How long the process lasts depends on your needs, the topics you work on and the goals you want to reach.

Does psychotherapy help?

Psychotherapy is not a quick or universal solution, but it can help you understand yourself, your patterns and the way you relate to yourself and others. Change takes time and active participation in the process.

How do I find a good psychotherapist?

Alongside training and expertise, the relationship you build with your therapist matters. It is important that you feel safe enough to speak openly and that their approach suits you.

What if I do not like the therapist, or feel uncomfortable?

A relationship with a therapist is built over time, and some discomfort can be a natural part of talking about personal, difficult things. Still, it is important that you feel safe enough to speak openly.

If something in the relationship with your therapist does not work for you, try telling them. Talking about it can be an important part of the therapy process.

If even after that conversation you do not feel you can build a relationship of trust, it is fine to look for another therapist. Sometimes you need to talk to several before you find the person you can work well with.

The goal

What is the goal of psychotherapy?

The goal of psychotherapy is not to become someone else, nor for someone to tell you how to live. The goal is to understand yourself better: your needs, feelings, boundaries, patterns of behaviour and the ways you build relationships with others. Through that process you gain room to live more authentically.

Authenticity means living in line with yourself: recognising what you really feel, what you need and what matters to you, instead of automatically following other people's expectations or patterns you picked up over time. It does not mean we will always know what we want. It means we have more awareness and more freedom to choose how we live.
